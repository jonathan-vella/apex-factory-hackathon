## 🔍 Context

> “The private-endpoint requirement forces Premium tiers on Service Bus and ACR. Service Bus Premium alone is 51% of the workload cost.” (02-architecture-assessment.md)

The approved architecture explicitly requires private endpoints for the registry and messaging workload, and the shared governance posture requires those endpoints to be private-only. This removes public access from the service and makes the storage and messaging layer compliant with the landing zone. A lower service tier would not satisfy the private endpoint requirement, so the design choice is not about throughput alone; it is a policy-driven operation.

## ✅ Decision

Deploy Azure Container Registry Premium and Service Bus Premium, with private endpoints enforced for both services.

## 🔄 Alternatives Considered

| Option | Pros | Cons | WAF Impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium ACR + Premium Service Bus (selected) | Private endpoint support, compliance alignment | ACR Premium $50.69/month and Service Bus Premium (1 MU) $677.08/month (Step 2) | Security ↑, Cost ↓ |
| Lower ACR or Service Bus tiers | Lower monthly cost | Do not support private endpoints; fail the private-endpoint requirement | Non-compliant |
| Mixed tiering with selective public access | May reduce cost | Contradicts the approved private-only posture and the no-public-access baseline | Non-compliant |

## ⚖️ Consequences

### Positive

- Aligns with the approved governance baseline and landing-zone policy.
- Normal application traffic to ACR and Service Bus uses the private endpoints and the private DNS path.
- Both services keep `publicNetworkAccess: Disabled`.

### Negative

- Premium tier costs are materially higher than lower tiers.
- The required `az acr import` path relies on the `AzureServices` trusted-services bypass, which widens the registry's network rule set (ADR-0004). It is owner-accepted and unverified until the post-deployment import test succeeds; access is not widened further if the test fails.
- More attention is required for private DNS readiness and endpoint diagnostics.

### Neutral

- This is a policy-driven design decision, not an optimization decision for the app itself.

## 🏛️ WAF Pillar Analysis

| Pillar | Impact | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Security | ↑ | Private-only endpoints and private DNS directly reduce surface exposure and simplify boundary enforcement. |
| Reliability | ↑ | Premium ACR and Service Bus gain zone redundancy automatically in `swedencentral` at no extra cost (assessment). The platform remains training/demo with best-effort reliability and no availability target; Premium is chosen for private-endpoint support, not production demand. |
| Performance | → | The tier is driven by the policy requirement, not by throughput. |
| Cost | ↓ | Premium tiers increase spend ($50.69 and $677.08 per month), but they are the only compliant path. |
| Operations | ↓ | Four private endpoints need private DNS readiness checks and an `AllMetrics` diagnostic setting each. |

## 🔒 Compliance Considerations

- This is a direct response to the landing-zone mandate for private endpoints and no public network access on PaaS data services.
- The design keeps the public network access disabled and relies on centralized private DNS and identity-based access.
- It is consistent with the no-public-blob and Entra-only security baseline for the workload.

## 📝 Implementation Notes

- Use the Premium ACR for the private registry and trusted-services bypass required for the import path.
- Use the Premium Service Bus namespace and queue pattern for private endpoint access and queueing.
- Confirm private DNS resolution and endpoint diagnostics as part of the post-deploy readiness checks.
